title: Photosynthesis

Photosynthesis is the process by which plants, algae and some bacteria convert light energy into chemical energy stored in sugars. In plants it takes place in chloroplasts. The overall equation is 6CO2 + 6H2O + light energy -> C6H12O6 + 6O2.

The light-dependent reactions occur in the thylakoid membranes. They capture light with pigments such as chlorophyll, split water molecules and produce ATP and NADPH. The oxygen released by plants comes from the splitting of water, not from carbon dioxide. The light-independent reactions, known as the Calvin cycle, occur in the stroma. There the enzyme RuBisCO fixes carbon dioxide, and ATP and NADPH are used to build sugars. Chlorophyll absorbs mainly blue and red light and reflects green light, which is why leaves look green.
