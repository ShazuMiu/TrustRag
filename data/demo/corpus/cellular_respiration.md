Cellular respiration is the process by which cells break down glucose to release energy in the form of ATP. In eukaryotic cells most of it takes place in the mitochondria. The overall equation is C6H12O6 + 6O2 -> 6CO2 + 6H2O + energy, which is roughly the reverse of photosynthesis.

Respiration has three main stages. Glycolysis happens in the cytoplasm, the Krebs cycle happens in the mitochondrial matrix, and the electron transport chain is located in the inner mitochondrial membrane. Oxygen is the final electron acceptor and is reduced to water. Aerobic respiration yields about 30 to 32 molecules of ATP per molecule of glucose.
